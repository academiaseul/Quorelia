A battery plant reported 202% round-trip efficiency.

Physically impossible. Energy out cannot exceed energy in.

The cause wasn't the batteries. It was a line in the equipment catalogue.

The SCADA system listed 1,776 racks. The plant physically has 888. Every rack was published twice — once over OPC-UA, once over ModBus — a detail buried in a footnote of the manufacturer's manual.

Sum the catalogue as published, and you double every energy figure in the report.

Here's what makes this worth talking about: nothing about that report would have looked wrong. The formatting was clean. The charts rendered. The efficiency number sat in a table looking exactly as authoritative as every other number on the page.

A second finding from the same plant: in a sample of 100 data channels, 52 returned nothing — with no error flag. Success: true, empty response.

How you classify that silence decides the entire report.

Count it as unavailable → a catastrophically low, false figure.
Count it as available → indefensible under audit.
Count it as unobserved and exclude it → the only honest answer.

The same day's data produced 54.41% availability under one treatment and 99.297% under the correct one.

Silence is not unavailability. It's the absence of evidence, and it has to be reported as exactly that.

Anyone can build a dashboard showing green numbers. The harder engineering problem is a system that blocks itself when it cannot substantiate what it's about to say.

That's what we built Quorelia to do.
